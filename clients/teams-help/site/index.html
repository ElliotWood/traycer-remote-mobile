Help

Ask an agent for something, from the chat you already have open.

Traycer runs agents on a machine you own. This bot is a way to reach them from Teams — see what they are doing, answer them when they are stuck, and start an assessment of a customer document. It is a front door, not the product.

  1. You ask the bot, attaching the customer's document.
  2. The bot confirms which product and which kind of request it thinks you meant.
  3. An assessment starts and runs for a while on your own machine.
  4. A review copy is produced for the bid team.
  5. A named human authorises it and lodges it. The bot does neither.
  6. Only then does anything reach the customer.
Before you start

Three things have to be true, and none of them are self-service.

If the bot answers “Couldn’t verify who you are” or “Access denied”, it is one of these — not something you can fix by rephrasing.

1. Installed for you personally

The app installs into a one-to-one chat with the bot. There is no channel or group-chat version, so @-mentioning it in a team channel does nothing — the message never reaches it.

2. Your account is linked to a host

Whoever set this up has to add your account to the registry. Until they do, every command that touches a host answers “Your account isn’t linked to a Traycer host yet.”

3. Your host is awake and signed in

The agents run on your machine, not in the cloud. If it is asleep, the bot can see nothing. Sign in there with traycer login.

Everything is scoped to one epic. The bot answers about the epic this conversation is bound to. Send epics to list them and epic <id> to pick one. A binding is held in memory, so it can be forgotten if the bot restarts — if answers suddenly look empty, re-bind before assuming something is broken.
The headline journey

Assessing a customer RFP or RFI

The journey the bot is built around. Some of it works today and some of it is designed but not yet connected — each step says which, because a help page that hides the difference just sends you to try something that answers with a help card.

1

Ask, in your own words

Partly live

Open the bot’s chat and describe what you have. It reads plain language — you do not need a command.

SL
@Traycer what about SensorMine for this one? Northgate-RFP-2026.pdf

The file is not read yet. Attaching it tells the bot the request is probably an opportunity, which changes what it offers — but the document itself does not reach the agent, and the agent is told “No documents were attached.” Paste the important parts into the message for now.

Ask a fully specific question — “does this fit SensorMine?” — and you currently get the general help card instead. Naming the product without a phrase like “RFP” or “does this fit” is what opens the flow below.

2

It checks it understood you

Live

Rather than guessing, the bot names what it thinks you want and waits. Two products are recognised — SensorMine and DR Migrate — and only a SensorMine opportunity has an assessment skill behind it today.

T
Traycer now
Before I start
Looks like a new SensorMine opportunity. Is that right?
Yes, go ahead Something else

Something else is not a dead end — it replies “No problem — tell me what you’d like me to do with it,” and you can rephrase.

3

The assessment starts, and you get a link

Live

Pressing Yes, go ahead creates a new agent on your host and hands it the request. This takes a few seconds — the card below replaces the waiting, and the work then runs for far longer than the conversation does.

T
Traycer now
Assessment started
what about SensorMine for this one?
It’s running — open it to watch progress. I’ll reply here when it’s done.
Watch progress

If the card says “Couldn’t confirm it started”, ask again exactly the same way. The request is de-duplicated, so repeating it cannot start a second assessment. If it says “I haven’t started”, nothing was created at all.

4

It replies again when the work is done

Not wired yet

The card above promises a follow-up. That follow-up does not exist yet — nothing sends it. Until it does, check on the work yourself: press Watch progress, or send fleet and open the agent from there.

When it does land, it will fire on the bundle being assembled from an authorised bid — not on the chat finishing. Those are different events, and treating “the agent stopped talking” as “the work is approved” is the mistake the design is specifically avoiding.

5

A human authorises and lodges

By design, outside Teams

This step is not missing and will not be added. It is the point of the whole design.

The one thing to take away

What the bot gives you

A review copy.

  • — Assembled for the bid team, not the buyer.
  • — Every claim in it is a draft until a person says otherwise.
  • — Labelled as a review copy, naming the human who authorised it.
What only a person does

Decides, authorises, lodges.

  • — The go / no-go call on whether to bid at all.
  • — Signing off each evidence claim that goes to a buyer.
  • — Actually lodging the tender.

A reply containing documents is not permission to send them to the customer. Rendering a bundle is not lodging a tender, and the bot has no way to authorise one — it holds read-only access to the bid and there is no button anywhere that changes that.

Two endings, both of them correct

Bid

You get documents

The assessment found a fit worth pursuing. Once a human has authorised the claims, a bundle is assembled and the bot delivers a review copy of it.

No-bid

You get no documents at all

The assessment concluded this one is not worth bidding, so no bundle is ever produced. You get the reasoning instead. This is a successful run, not a failure, and it is expected to be a common one. Nothing is broken and there is nothing to retry.

Day to day

Seven things you can type

You can also just ask in plain language. These are the shortcuts when you know what you want. Ids come from fleet — you never have to memorise one.

fleet
The agents in this epic, newest and most active first. Up to twelve, each with an Open button — tapping the row does the same thing. Everything you can do to an agent lives one level down, on the card Open takes you to.
chat <id>
One agent’s status, with anything waiting on you pulled above the fold, its last three messages, and a box to reply. This card carries the Reply and History buttons.
log <id>
Its history, five messages at a time, newest first. Page with ↑ Older and ↓ Newer.
say <id> <message>
Send an agent a message. Leave the message off and you get a compose box instead, which is easier for anything long.
epics
Your epics, so you can bind one to this conversation.
epic <id>
Bind this conversation to an epic. Send it bare and it tells you the syntax.
help
The starting card. An empty message does the same thing.
Ids are long; you only need the start. The cards show a shortened form and the buttons carry the full one, so the reliable move is always to run fleet and press a button rather than to type an id by hand. If you do mistype one, the bot answers “That doesn’t look like a chat.”

Approvals

The agent wants to do something and is waiting for a yes. Rejecting takes an optional reason, and the reason is sent to the agent — so it is worth writing.

The buttons stay on the card after you press them. That is a Teams limitation, not a failed press: look for the Approved or Rejected card that appears underneath.

Send answers

Interviews

A set of questions the agent needs answered before it can carry on. Every question has to be filled in before it will send.

Some interviews cannot be answered from Teams and say so — the questions did not reach the bot. Those have to be answered on the desktop.

If a reply says it couldn’t confirm, do not press again. An unconfirmed approval or answer may well have landed. Re-sending is a second real instruction the agent will act on, not a harmless retry. Run chat <id> first — if the question is gone from the list, it went through.
When it breaks

What the bot says, and what it means

Most failures are your host being unreachable rather than anything you typed. The bot deliberately never shows raw errors, so these headlines are the diagnosis.

Couldn’t reach your Traycer host
Your machine is asleep, offline, or the host process is not running. Wake it and try again. Nothing was changed.
Your host isn’t signed in
Run traycer login on the machine running your host.
Your host sign-in expired
Send the same command again — it refreshes on its own. This one genuinely does fix itself on a retry.
Your host took too long to answer
It went over twenty seconds. Usually a busy or waking machine. Nothing was changed.
Host unreachable
Shown in place of a status. The card cannot be refreshed, so it is not shown at all rather than showing you something stale.
Read-only from here
You can read this agent but not message it — it runs on a host you cannot reach. Nothing is wrong; it is somebody else’s machine.
Not quite
A command was almost right. The sentence underneath says which part. Rephrasing in plain language usually works better than fixing the syntax.
This deployment can’t start assessments yet
Not your fault and not fixable from Teams — the deployment is missing the configuration that says which host to start assessments on. Tell whoever runs it.
One thing worth knowing about attribution. A message or an interview answer sent from Teams is recorded in the agent’s transcript under the host owner’s name, not yours. If several people use the same host, the transcript cannot tell you who actually pressed the button.
